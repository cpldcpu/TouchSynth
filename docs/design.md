# TouchSynth Poly: design document

Status: 0.2 (2026-10-18). This document states what the synth must do, how the system is divided and why, the
budgets, how each requirement is verified, and what is still open. Circuit values, pin maps and connector
pinouts are in [spec.md](spec.md); the history of the decisions is in [devlog.md](devlog.md).

## 1. Scope

A polyphonic analog synthesizer with 4 to 8 voices, played and edited from a computer over USB or from a USB-MIDI
keyboard. Each voice is an identical plug-in card with its own microcontroller, digital oscillators and an analog
filter and VCA (TouchSynth rev 0.1's circuits at 5 V). A backplane holds the USB controller, the voice bus, the
power and the stereo mixer.

Not in scope: a keyboard or front panel on the instrument (it is played from the computer or a MIDI keyboard), an
analog DCO, an audio codec, a voltage-controlled reconstruction filter, temperature compensation of the expo
converters (calibration covers drift), ID pins on the cards.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Partitioning

```
 computer ──USB-MIDI/CDC──┐                         ┌── voice card 1 ── AUDIO_OUT ──┐
 MIDI keyboard ──USB host─┤                         ├── voice card 2 ── AUDIO_OUT ──┤
                          ▼                         │        ...                     ▼
                 ┌─────────────────┐  voice bus     │                       ┌──────────────┐
                 │ CH32V203        │──RX/TX/SYNC────┤                       │ stereo mixer │──► line out L/R
                 │ USB, allocation │──NRST, SWIO x8─┤                       │              │──► ADCs (USB audio)
                 └─────────────────┘                └── voice card 8 ───────┴──────────────┘
                    8 MHz crystal: master clock for every voice's pitch
```

| Block | Responsibility | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| Controller (backplane) | USB device and host, MIDI parsing, voice allocation, patch storage, bus master, clock reference, programming the cards | the only crystal; USB needs one; one place for the whole instrument's state |
| Voice card MCU | oscillators, envelopes, modulation, CV generation, self-calibration | one MCU per voice: 48 MHz is enough for one 3-oscillator voice, not for two (budget 4.1) |
| Voice card analog | reconstruction filter, expo converter, ladder, VCA, loopback | the analog sound of rev 0.1; local references make the card independent of backplane ground |
| Mixer (backplane) | sum, pan, line out, ADC feed | one stage for all cards, at the quiet end of the backplane |

Main design choices and their basis:

- **Digital oscillators on a PWM DAC instead of the analog DCO:** on board 1 the DAC and the DCO saw measure the
  same; the DAC gives three oscillators, any waveform and the drums without extra parts (S9, spec 1).
- **187.5 kHz carrier, one sample per period, second-order error feedback:** images, not bit depth, limit the
  quality; at this rate the 7-bit DAC reaches an ideal DAC's quality in the model (S9b), and a 30 kHz 2-pole
  filter takes the carrier to −74 dB (S11).
- **Single 5 V:** rail-to-rail MCP6002s replace the TL072s; simulated within 1–2 dB of rev 0.1 at 3.6 mA (S10).
- **UART broadcast + SYNC instead of SPI:** SPI receive DMA would share a channel with the DAC (spec 5).
- **Synchronised HSI instead of a clock line:** each card trims its oscillator to the SYNC pulses; a shared clock
  remains as a fitted-on-demand fallback (spec 6).

### 3.2 Clock domains

| Domain | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Controller | 8 MHz crystal, PLL 144 MHz | USB, bus baud rate, SYNC 1 kHz, 24 MHz fallback clock (TIM3 CH4) |
| Each voice | own HSI 24 MHz, ×2 PLL, trimmed to SYNC | DAC carrier 187.5 kHz, CV PWM 46.9 kHz, oscillator phase steps scaled by the measured SYNC ratio |

The voice carriers are not phase locked to each other; their offsets after trim (up to one HSITRIM step) only
matter if carrier residue from several cards meets a nonlinearity, which the filters prevent (spec 6).

### 3.3 Operating sequence

1. **Power-up:** the controller holds every NRST low and releases one slot at a time.
2. **Per slot:** the released card measures SYNC, sets HSITRIM, derives its baud divider, listens on RX and gets
   its address; a slot that does not answer is empty. A card without SYNC falls into standalone mode (spec 3.4).
3. **Calibration (on request or at power-up):** each card runs its loopback calibration and reports through the
   return line; the controller keeps the results with the patch data.
4. **Play:** MIDI events become voice messages (note, pitch, gate, parameters) on the broadcast line; the cards
   keep tracking SYNC for their pitch.
5. **Update:** the controller reprograms a card over its SWIO line with an image received over CDC.

## 5. Interfaces

### 5.1 Card to backplane

2×8 pin header (spec 3.3): +5 V ×2, GND ×4, AUDIO_OUT + AUDIO_GND (pseudo-differential into the mixer), SYNC, RX,
TX (open drain), NRST, SWIO, CLK_IN (fallback clock), one spare. All logic at 3.3 V.

### 5.2 Voice bus

Protocol proposal: [bus.md](bus.md). In short:

- **Frames:** SYNC's rising edges at 1 kHz from the controller's crystal; every card runs its control tick on them,
  so envelopes and LFOs advance in lockstep, and scales its oscillators to the measured frame period (pitch locked
  to the crystal).
- **Timed events:** every note and parameter message carries the frame in which it executes; the controller sends
  it 2 frames ahead (bus lead), so bursts spread over the bus without changing their timing.
- **Presentation delay:** what a card computes in frame n reaches its DAC and CV outputs at frame n + D, the DAC
  switch exact to one carrier period. With 256-period DAC buffer halves D = 4 frames.
- **Packets:** COBS-framed, voice bitmask address, CRC-8; broadcast without acknowledgements, lost events repaired by
  periodic voice-state and patch refreshes; the return line carries replies to one request at a time.
- **Enumeration:** slot by slot over NRST, address = slot, the card reports its chip ID.

### 5.3 Latency and synchronisation

Total latency from a MIDI event to sound: 6–7 ms (bus lead 2 frames, presentation delay 4 frames, plus the USB
frame), the same for every event and every voice. Alignment between cards: a few microseconds (SYNC timestamp
jitter, one carrier period, the frame-period prediction). With the current 1024-period buffer halves the delay would
be 12 frames (14–15 ms); the shorter halves also free 3 KB of RAM.

### 5.4 USB

Composite full-speed device on USBD: USB-MIDI (class compliant), CDC (configuration, presets, firmware, a
bridge to each card's console), UAC1 audio input as an option. USB host on USBFS for class-compliant MIDI
keyboards. Stack: ch32fun USB examples or a TinyUSB port (open).

## 7. Status and open points

### 7.1 Ready

- Architecture, voice card circuit values (spec 3.1), pin map (spec 3.2), connector pinouts (spec 3.3), DMA plan.
- Voice firmware's oscillator engine and DAC output (on board 1, from the rev 0.1 firmware).

### 7.2 Not ready

- **Voice card:** schematic done and checked; PCB generated and routed (50 × 50 mm, 4 layers, DRC clean,
  `hw/voice/voice.kicad_pcb`). Pending: the stereo pan stage (S13), then review and order.
- **Backplane schematic:** not started.
- **Calibration tables for 5 V:** the firmware and the behavioural model still use rev 0.1's measured data;
  new tables come from the first card.

### 7.3 Decisions needed

| Topic | Question | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Card holding | header only, card guides, or an enclosure with slots | header only for the first build |
| Pan positions | fixed per slot (resistor values), or all centre | spread from left to right over the 8 slots |
| CH32V203 assembly | own stock, hand-soldered, or JLCPCB assembly | own stock, hand-soldered (as the voice MCUs) |

Decided (2026-10-18): cards about 60 × 45 mm with 4 layers, 10 mm slot pitch, backplane 2 layers within
100 × 100 mm, no controls on the instrument (USB only); multitimbral (the more flexible option); a compact
first program set, extended later; patches as parameter sets stored on the controller (midi.md).

### 7.4 Design and firmware work

- Voice card: S13 pan stage into schematic and PCB, review, order, then one card built (V2).
- Voice firmware: a voice card board definition before the first card is powered. The firmware still uses
  board 1's pin map, which drives PD4 (SYNC_IN on the card) and PC4 (ADC_AUDIO, loading the loopback) push-pull,
  reads the loopback on PD5 (the card's TX) and runs USART1 receive-only (spec 3.2 pin map; found in the schematic
  review). Then: tunes as a build option, the DCO code out, fixed-point kettle, bus receiver, SYNC measurement and
  HSITRIM, standalone fallback, calibration for the 5 V core, the patch parameters of midi.md.
- Bus protocol: proposal in bus.md; to implement on board 1 first (a second board or a V203 dev board as
  controller), with the 256-period buffer halves.
- DAC loading once per period (format 4, dual: format 3; spec 3.2) in the voice firmware, with a safe start
  sequence; measured on board 1.
- Backplane schematic and layout: V203, USB-C device, USB-A host with a current-limited switch, mixer, slot row.
- Controller firmware: USB stack (MIDI + CDC first, host second, audio last), voice allocation, SWIO programmer.

### 7.5 Risks

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| CPU left after the oscillators (about 30 %) is not enough for envelopes, modulation and the bus | fewer oscillators or lower control rate | half-rate mode (−28 %, measured); measure early on board 1 with the bus receiver running |
| Short buffer halves underrun when the main loop stalls (board 1: gaps of 4–10 ms, from the console and UI) | clicks | done: rendering in the DMA interrupt, 0 overruns (bus.md 3.1) |
| A modal (kettle) strike computes its coefficients in soft float in the tick (8.5 ms) | DAC dropouts at each strike | fixed-point coefficients, or computed outside the tick |
| RAM (8 KB) with a 4 KB DAC ring | no room for the bus buffers or the dual-PWM format | 256-period buffer halves (1 KB, also sets the latency); remove the rev 0.1 UI |
| HSI wander between voices audible on unison patches | slight beating | it resembles analog VCOs; the shared-clock fallback (CLK_IN) removes it |
| 5 V core differs from the simulation (Microchip's model lacks the input transition and distortion detail) | resonance, THD or offsets off | bench check on the first card before building more |
| Carrier residue from 8 unsynchronised cards beats in the mixer | audible tones | −74 dB per card at the line out; check in V4 |
